# Online Dating Protocol

A complete, evidence-labelled protocol for online dating. It turns a person's real life into a profile that gets noticed, a messaging routine that gets to a date fast, and a weekly review loop that improves results with their own data.

The protocol was built and tested for men seeking women on Tinder and Instagram, which is also where most of the available data comes from. It adapts to other apps and audiences, but say so when a number comes from a different population than the user's.

## Operating principles

1. **The profile is a landing page.** First photo = hero image. First line of text = hook. Other photos and prompts = supporting info. Group photo = social proof. Bio or prompt question = call to action.
2. **Photos carry the result.** In one study, +1.5 points of photo attractiveness gave +20 % matches, versus +2 % for an equally improved bio. Spend time and money on photos first.
3. **Activity beats money.** Tinder says its most important ranking factor is using the app. No paid tier fixes a weak profile.
4. **Messages organise; meetings seduce.** About 6-7 messages from the user between match and date. When in doubt, write less.
5. **Stand out, don't become someone else.** Specific, true details beat generic claims. If a line could apply to 1,000 other people, it is a 1-in-1,000 shot.
6. **80/20.** Focus on the 20 % of actions that drive 80 % of results; don't fixate on exceptions.
7. **Measure, change one thing at a time.** The user's own weekly numbers outrank every study in this file.
8. **Honesty is non-negotiable.** No lying about age, height, location or job. No AI-generated or face-altering photos. No manipulation scripts.

## How to run this skill

Work in the user's language and localise every example (places, food, culture) to where they live. Never put personal details of the user into anything meant to be shared publicly.

### Step 0 — Intake

Ask only what you can't infer, ideally in one round:

- Apps used now or planned (Tinder, Hinge, Bumble, Instagram, other) and how heavily they've been used before (months, intensity, results).
- What they're looking for: relationship, open with no pressure, casual, mostly social.
- Location, how far they're willing to travel, whether they split time between places or plan to move.
- Age, height, the age range they want.
- Work, passions, skills, sports, a few true anecdotes. Anything visual and unusual (an instrument, a sport, a craft, a local landscape).
- Constraints: professional boundaries (clients, students, patients), small community, privacy.
- Current material: screenshots of the current profile, Instagram top grid, every usable photo, and an export of their app data if possible (Tinder: Settings → download my data).

## Evidence rules

Label every number with its tier and never invent one.

| Tier | What it is | How to present it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research | Peer-reviewed or academic study | Cite authors, year, sample; mention limits (lab setting, students, one country) |
| Platform data | Published by Tinder, Hinge, Match, OkCupid | Cite and date it; note it's a correlation from a company with a product to sell |
| Aggregated user data | e.g. SwipeStats (users upload their Tinder exports) | Cite; note self-selection bias |
| Practitioner | Coaches, courses, books | Present as experience-based advice, not proof |
| Unverified | Figure with no traceable primary source | Say "unverified" explicitly, or drop it |

When the user brings new data that contradicts the current advice, say so plainly ("this changes my earlier recommendation on X"), update the protocol, and explain the reason in one line.

## 1. How the apps rank you

**Tinder (official):** "Elo is old news". Tinder no longer relies on an Elo desirability score. Many courses and articles still describe Elo; treat that as outdated. What Tinder says it uses:

- **Activity** — the most important factor. It prioritises people who are active, and active at the same time.
- **Distance** and basic preferences (age, gender, radius).
- **Reactions** — how often your profile is liked or passed, relative to profiles in your area. A profile that collected many passes starts with a handicap, which is why first impressions matter.
- **Content** — interests, lifestyle and anonymised photo cues used to suggest profiles similar to ones you like.
- Not used, per Tinder: social status, religion, ethnicity.

**Practitioner claims (unverified by the apps):** indiscriminate right-swiping may lower your ranking; a fresh profile may get a temporary visibility boost. Don't build strategy on either.

**Features to know (as of 2026; check availability by country):**

- **Smart Photos** — Tinder tests your photos and puts the best performer first; up to +12 % matches in Tinder's tests.
- **Spotify Anthem + Music Mode** — more than half of US matches involve at least one person with an Anthem (Tinder, March 2026). Music Mode prioritises shared music taste.
- **Face Check** (mandatory liveness check, expanding country by country) and **photo verification** badge; in some European countries an optional ID + video-selfie verification.
- **Chemistry** — one AI-curated daily recommendation, with optional camera-roll analysis (launched in Australia/NZ, then US/Canada).
- **Double Date** — go out as two pairs with a friend.
- **Photo Enhance**, **Visual Interests**, **Events**, **video speed dating** — announced or piloting.
- Hinge, Bumble and others use similar engagement signals (activity, likes and passes, responsiveness, profile completeness).

## 2. Reset protocol

| Situation of the current account | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Heavy use for 6+ months with poor results | Full reset |
| Light or irregular use | No reset: rebuild the profile in place and restart daily activity |
| Account younger than ~2 months | No reset |

**Full reset, done once, properly:**

1. Download your data first (baseline match rate).
2. Delete the account inside the app, then delete the app.
3. Wait **at least 3 months**. Tinder's privacy policy keeps data for a 3-month safety window after closure (1 year after a ban), and a new account created with the same credentials within 90 days may have data restored.
4. Optional: request deletion of your data (GDPR right to erasure in the EU/UK, CCPA in California).
5. Use the waiting time to build photos and words, so the new account is complete on day one.

No repeated delete/recreate cycles: Tinder uses safety data to stop banned or abusive users from coming back. **Alternative to waiting:** most people use more than one app; launch the new profile on a different app meanwhile.

## 3. Photos

### What the evidence says

- **The face is judged in about 100 ms**; longer viewing barely changes the judgement (Willis & Todorov, 2006). The first photo decides most outcomes.
- **People pick worse photos of themselves than strangers do**, including for dating sites (White, Sutherland & Burton, 2017, 600+ participants). Never let the user choose alone.
- **Smile.** For men, smiling helps trust most (+2.55/10 for an open-mouth smile, Photofeeler) but moves attractiveness less (< 0.5 point). Hinge found men smiling *without* teeth got 43 % more likes than with teeth. A broad happy smile reads as less sexually attractive in men than a proud, confident expression (Tracy & Beall, 2011, 1,000+ participants). **Rule: hero photo = closed-mouth smile, direct gaze; big laughs go on secondary photos.**
- **Gaze.** Smiles are more attractive with direct gaze; with averted gaze, a neutral face does better (Jones et al., 2006). Hinge: looking at the camera +102 % likes for men versus looking away. The only clearly bad combo is no smile + no eye contact (Photofeeler replication of OkCupid; OkCupid's "don't smile, look away" advice did not replicate).
- **Alone vs group.** Faces look more attractive in a group (cheerleader effect, Walker & Vul), but Hinge found men alone in the photo got 11 % more likes than in group shots. One group photo, never first, user at the centre.
- **Facial hair.** ~10-day stubble rated most attractive by 351 women; 5-day stubble least (Dixson & Brooks, 2013). Test it rather than assume.
- **Instruments.** A 2013 French field study reported 31 % phone numbers with a guitar case vs 14 % with no bag, but its feasibility has been seriously questioned. A study of 11,543 Swedish twins found musical ability doesn't increase number of partners (Mosing et al., 2015). A skill attracts attention and starts conversations; it doesn't replace good photos.

**Photo effects on likes (Hinge, 2017 analysis; platform data):**

| Element | Effect on likes |
| --- | --- |
| Black-and-white photo (only 3 % of photos) | +106 % |
| Looking at the camera (men, vs looking away) | +102 % |
| Night out / social setting | +74 % |
| Doing a sport (men) | +45 % |
| Smile without teeth (men, vs with teeth) | +43 % |
| Visible smile | +23 % |
| Alone (men, vs group) | +11 % |
| Selfie | −40 % |
| Gym selfie (unverified figure) | −40 % |
| Sunglasses | −41 % |
| Bathroom selfie | −90 % |
| Snapchat-style filter | −90 % |
| Posing with someone who could be a partner | −98 % |

Other: same test profile got 54 % matches without a cigarette vs 29 % with one (ASH, 2014).

### Photo scorecard (score each photo out of 5)

| Criterion | Test |
| --- | --- |
| Quality | Sharp, well lit, not pixelated, readable full-screen on a phone (portrait mode on a recent phone is enough) |
| Face | Face is the centre of attention; she knows what he looks like in one second; not a stiff LinkedIn headshot |
| Eyes | Alive, "smiling" eyes; slight squint of the lower lid ("squinch", popularised by photographer Peter Hurley); direct gaze if smiling |
| Personal | Says something true about him (activity, personality, place) |
| Hook | Contains something she can comment on |

Hero photo: aim 5/5, minimum 4/5. Photos 2-6: minimum 3/5. Background matters: nature, views, art, architecture, a great wall; never a messy room.

### The six slots

| # | Role | What we want |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hero | Portrait, 4-5/5, direct gaze, closed-mouth smile, striking local background (last hour before sunset) |
| 2 | Signature activity | Doing the thing that defines him (instrument, craft, cooking), face visible, good light; strong candidate for black-and-white |
| 3 | Sport / full body | In action: hike, surf, climbing, martial art. Re-shoot when he's in his best shape |
| 4 | Social proof | Night out or friends, he is the star and clearly central |
| 5 | Second portrait | Different outfit and setting (urban, café, colourful wall) |
| 6 | Personality / warmth | Humour, a trait, family (a parent or grandparent is safe), kindness |

### Hard rules

- No two photos with the same outfit or location; max 2 photos from the same professional shoot.
- No selfies (bathroom or gym selfies especially), no filters, no AI photos, no face-altering "enhance".
- No sunglasses ideally, never on the hero photo.
- Never alone with a woman who could be read as a partner; family only if obvious.
- No photos with clients, students or minors. No playgrounds. Smoking and guns are polarising: avoid unless it's a deliberate filter.
- Shirtless only if candid and natural (beach, sport); staged shirtless reads try-hard.
- Solid or neutral colours, trusted outfits, authentic best self. Laughter and varied expressions on secondary photos.
- A photo with a yacht or a celebrity only helps if it also scores high.
- Fishing photos are fine if he genuinely loves fishing.

### Getting the photos (in this order)

1. **Sort what exists** with the scorecard; keep everything at 3/5 or above.
2. **DIY session**: a patient friend, or a tripod + Bluetooth remote. It's a numbers game: hundreds of frames for one great shot. Keep energy up (comedy in the earbuds). Fix the three common problems: insufficient light, low resolution, bad background.
3. **Photographer** (most recommended; budget roughly 150-400 locally, up to 1,000+ for dating specialists). Find one via Instagram, local photography groups, freelance platforms, photography students, photo-experience marketplaces.
   - Brief: "lifestyle photos for social media and dating apps". If they don't get it, don't hire.
   - Terms: 2-3 walkable locations, 2-3 outfits, 10+ edited photos.
   - Share reference photos (especially action shots of the signature activity); scout locations with Instagram place search.
   - Change haircut, beard or glasses *before* the shoot. Vet their portfolio: expensive ≠ good.
4. **Timing**: pick the season and light with the best backgrounds and outfits; look your personal best (training, sleep, a bit of sun), and have fun: it shows in the face.

### Validation

- Test candidates on Photofeeler or a similar rating service (practitioner threshold: 8/10 on every axis).
- Have a female friend review the final six in person.
- Free proxy: post a candidate on Instagram and compare likes and comments with the usual baseline.
- Once all six are 3/5+, turn on Smart Photos and let the data choose the order.

## 4. Bio, prompts and fields

### Structure

The classic written bio has four parts: **hook** (makes her double-take), **personal statement** (who you are, specific; a little flex is fine), **qualifier** (optional: who you get along with, specific and positive), **call to action** (a concrete next step she can take).

On Tinder, short bios win for men (SwipeStats, 6,235 male profiles): 1-50 characters 6.9 % match rate, 51-150 5.2 %, empty 5.3 %, 151-300 4.0 %, 300+ 4.0 %. So split the structure: **hook + call to action in the bio (under ~150 characters)**, personal statement and qualifier in prompts.

On Hinge, likes on text prompts were 47 % more likely to lead to a date than likes on photos (Hinge, 2024). Prompts are conversation entry points, not decoration.

### Writing rules

- Show, don't tell: make a joke instead of saying "I'm funny". Women value men who produce humour (Bressler et al., 2006).
- Specific details: what exactly, with whom, where, when. "Business owner" says nothing.
- When in doubt whether she'll "get it", simplify. Non-native speakers: get a native-speaker friend to check.
- Every word counts; an hour per sentence is a good investment.
- Keep it light: think about how she wants to feel meeting the right person.
- Sarcasm sparingly. No recycled meme or pickup-artist lines.
- Disqualifiers ("swipe left if…") read negative and insecure. A qualifier must be unique ("fun and adventurous" adds nothing).
- Visible trait she will notice anyway (short height, baldness, wheelchair): get ahead of it with self-deprecating humour. Her speculation is the problem, not the trait.

**Clichés to never write:** "I love to travel", "super competitive", "new to this app", "bad at introductions", "new in town", "fun-loving, genuine and kind", "looking for someone with a sense of humour".

### Templates (adapt to the user's real life and language)

- Challenge hook + CTA: "I make the best croissants in town. Bring me a better one and dinner's on me."
- Self-deprecating hook + CTA: "Climbing instructor. My mum still calls it a hobby. Tell me your biggest fear and I'll tell you how to beat it."
- Personal statement prompt ("The one thing you should know about me"): "By day I design bridges, by night I'm learning the saxophone. My neighbours are very patient people."
- Qualifier prompt ("We'll get along if"): "you'd rather see a small gig in a bar than a club."
- CTA prompt ("I bet you can't"): "name a pastry I can't make from scratch."

Useful prompt questions (wording varies by app): The way to win me over is · My simple pleasures · We'll get along if · Unusual skills · I bet you can't · The one thing you should know about me is · What if I told you that · I won't shut up about · A life goal of mine · Typical Sunday · First round is on me if · Together, we could.

### Structured fields

| Field | Recommendation | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Height | List it if 178 cm / 5'10" or more; tall men get more right swipes (unverified aggregator figures: −70 % matches under 5'9", +23 % right swipes over 6'0") | Height preferences are real but translate into real couples only modestly (Stulp et al., 2013) |
| Job title | Leave empty; put the job in the bio or a prompt if it's a hook | Listing a job correlates with 39 % fewer matches for men (SwipeStats) |
| Education | Leave empty | −37 % (SwipeStats) |
| School | Optional | ~+3 %, negligible |
| Company | Empty | Privacy; keeps businesses unsearchable |
| City | Where he actually lives | Only exceptions: moving there soon, or genuinely splitting time |
| Relationship goal | Honest; "long-term, open to short" reassures more than "still figuring it out" | Clarity beats vagueness |
| Interests | 5 concrete, true ones | Only activities he actually does |
| Drinking, smoking, politics, star sign | Empty unless it's a real filter (e.g. a smoker who only wants smokers) | Less is more |
| Instagram link | Only after the Instagram rebuild | ~−2 %, no real effect |
| Emojis in bio | Neutral | ~−2 %, no real effect |
| Spotify | Connected + Anthem | See section 5 |

Never lie about age: plenty of people prefer older partners, and lies backfire.

## 5. App settings and daily usage

**Choosing apps.** For most people the app matters less than the profile: profiles barely change across apps, good people are on all of them, and the best app varies by city. Place apps on two axes, intent (casual ↔ relationship) and specificity (everyone ↔ a niche). Exception: if the user will only date within a specific community (religion, culture, lifestyle), a niche app is far more efficient.

**Launch checklist**

- Photo verification badge on day one; ID verification where offered.
- Spotify connected with an Anthem that is itself a hook (a track he plays, or his own recording). Music Mode on if music matters to him.
- Radius sized to real travel (e.g. ~30 km in a dense area); widen when travelling.
- Broad age range at first (stated preferences barely predict real choices, Eastwick & Finkel 2008); adjust after 2 weeks.
- Smart Photos on once all six photos are 3/5+.
- Instagram unlinked until rebuilt.

**Daily discipline**

- Two sessions of 10-15 minutes a day, one in the evening. Day-of-week differences are tiny (2.44-2.54 % match rate for men by weekday); consistency matters more than timing.
- Short sessions also protect judgement: the more profiles people see in a row, the more they reject and the less satisfied they feel (Pronk & Denissen, 2020).
- **Selective likes, ~20-40 %:** only people he'd really have a drink with. Selective desire is more often reciprocated than desire for everyone (Eastwick et al., 2007). Read the bio before liking: that's where the first message comes from.
- Answer matches the same day.

**Paying**

| Option | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Plus / Gold / Platinum, Roses | No for the first 4 weeks on the rebuilt profile |
| Gold after 4 weeks | One test month is reasonable in a small market where seeing who liked you avoids missing matches |
| Boost | Only as a measured test (weeknight ~9 pm) |
| Super Like | Not a strategy; for a truly aligned profile |
| Voice, video, new gimmicks | Skip unless obviously right; easier to get wrong than right |

**Wellbeing.** Tinder users report lower body satisfaction, and male users lower self-esteem (Strübel & Petrie, correlational). Cap app time, judge the *profile* on weekly numbers, never self-worth on a swipe.

## 6. Messaging: from match to second date

### Benchmarks (men on Tinder, SwipeStats, 2.56 M matches)

Average match rate 5.3 %, median 2 %, top 10 % 12.5 %, bottom 10 % 0.3 % (women: median 41 %). Conversation depth of men's matches:

| Messages in the conversation | Share of matches |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 11.0 % |
| 1 | 32.4 % |
| 2-5 | 30.7 % |
| 6-10 | 10.9 % |
| 11-20 | 7.7 % |
| 21-50 | 5.1 % |
| 50+ | 2.1 % |

43 % of matches die before a second message; only ~1 in 4 passes 5 messages. On Tinder, the median man's message is 12 characters and 63 % of men write within 5 minutes (Tyson et al., 2016): a thoughtful two-part opener already stands out.

### The sequence (6-7 messages from him before the date)

| Stage | His message | Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Match | Icebreaker: hook + question | 1 |
| Her first reply | Connection message | 1-2 |
| Connection made | Suggest a specific activity + ask for her number | 1 |
| Number received | Remind who he is + invite with day and place; then lock the time | 2 |
| Day of the date | Confirmation, 10 am-12 pm for an evening date | 1 |

Met in person (a gig, a club, a party): 3-4 messages. Send a short intro text right away ("Alex here, we met at the climbing gym. I'll message you this week about that coffee :)"); it removes any expectation of an immediate conversation. Then invite, then confirm.

### Timing

Reply within 24 hours, ideally within 6 hours of matching (SwipeStats: +61 % replies within 6 h; a "+50 % within 24 h" figure circulates without a source). Don't play waiting games.

### Icebreakers: a hook that makes her smile + a question

What works in first messages (OkCupid, 500,000+ messages): referencing her profile is the strongest lever; specific interests (a band, a tattoo, a dish) beat generalities; a light tone helps ("haha" ~45 % reply rate); mild self-deprecation helps men. What hurts: "hey/hi" alone (~22 % replies vs ~32 % average, per SwipeStats citing OkCupid) and physical compliments ("gorgeous", "sexy": under 20 %). Personalised messages on shared interests are reported at ~58 % reply rate (secondary source). Long openers don't increase reply odds (Bruch & Newman, 2018). 60 % of singles say they're more likely to answer a funny message. On Hinge, a like sent with a comment roughly doubles the chance of a date, and 72 % of users say a message with a like makes them more inclined to consider someone (Hinge). GIFs are reported to get 30 % more replies (SwipeStats, secondary).

| What's on her profile | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A recognisable place | "That looks like {PLACE}!" + your experience + question | "That looks like {volcano}! I hiked it at sunrise last year. Did you do the crater rim?" |
| Food or a drink | "That looks so good" + your best address or secret technique | "That curry looks incredible. Have you tried {PLACE}? Best in town for me." |
| An outfit or costume | Playful nickname + question | "Hello captain, nice hat. Special occasion or everyday look?" |
| A pet | Compliment a detail + tease | "Your dog is adorable, love the bandana. Is he yours, or did you borrow him to win at dating apps? ;)" |
| None of these | "Let me guess" mini-story about her | "Let me guess: you moved here for a one-year job, fell in love with the place, and you've been postponing the return for three years. How did I do?" |
| Her music | Turn his own hook toward her | "Your Anthem tells me you have good taste. What should I listen to next?" |

Nothing to say even after looking? Don't message. Never: "Hey beautiful", "What's up?", "How was your weekend?".

**Signature move.** Build one from the user's real skill. A musician: once on WhatsApp or Instagram, a 15-second voice note playing her song in his style. A cook: a photo of the dish she mentioned. Nobody else can copy it.

### Connection message (1-2 maximum)

1. Acknowledge what she said (optional) so she feels heard.
2. Share a detail about himself so she wants to meet him.
3. Ask an open question. "Do you surf?" gets yes/no; "I saw you surf, what got you started?" gets a story. Asking more questions, especially follow-ups, increases liking (Huang et al., 2017).
4. Match her length, never write twice as much, and send one message rather than a burst: back-to-back texts cut likability by ~26 % among under-40s compared with one paragraph (Lyngo Lab, 805 participants).

Questions that lead naturally to an invite: plans ("Anything fun planned for the holidays?" → a drink before she leaves), places ("Ever been to {PLACE}?" → an offer to go), her life ("So what do you actually do all day?" → "Tell me the rest over a drink").

### Leaving the app and inviting

1. **Exit by his 3rd or 4th message**, or as soon as the tone is good: an activity that flows from the conversation + the number. "You haven't been to {PLACE}? Let's fix that. Drop me your number and I'll text you a plan." The date gives her a reason to share her number. Chatting too long online leads to idealisation and disappointment; the link between online duration and first-date quality is curvilinear (Ramirez et al., 2015).
2. **No number?** Many women (~30 %, practitioner estimate) prefer to share it after a first date. Fine: schedule on the app. "Coffee sounds great. Thursday afternoon?"
3. **First text**, later that day or the next, not back on the app: "Hi {name}, {his name} here (from Tinder). {PLACE} next week? Wednesday or Thursday works for me." She may have several matches with his name.
4. She says yes: "Perfect, see you there at 7. I'll confirm on the day." Then nothing until the day.
5. **Day of**, late morning: "Morning {name}! We're still good for {PLACE} at 7 tonight?"
6. She hesitates to meet: suggest a short call instead of more texting. Don't become a pen pal.

### Reading her interest (digital body language)

| Signal | Strong interest | Weak interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Balance | She asks questions back, develops, relaunches | One-word answers ("Yeah", "Cool"); he carries everything |
| Length | Long messages, repeated letters ("sooo good!!") | Abbreviations, "ok", "k" |
| Timing | She writes first; a good-morning message | Repeated slow, dry replies |
| Punctuation | Exclamation marks, emojis | Full stop on a short reply ("Yes."), read as less sincere (Klin et al., Binghamton, 2015) |

Two weak exchanges in a row: invite once; no clear answer, move on. For himself: no full stop on short replies, 1-3 emojis per message at most (Match Singles in America: 52 % of emoji users had a first date that year vs 27 % of non-users; correlation only). Early on, don't over-signal interest: in one small study women were more attracted to men whose interest was uncertain (Whitchurch et al., 2011). Be clear about the invitation, restrained about the enthusiasm.

### The first date

- 60-90 minutes, public, low budget, easy to extend if it goes well; near both, or halfway in car-dependent areas.
- Ideal terrain: a bar or café with live music, a walk at sunset. From the second date, a new, slightly stimulating activity (short hike, concert, kayak) beats another dinner; shared novel activities raise relationship quality in couples (Aron et al., 2000).
- Ask questions, follow up ("and then?", "what did you love about it?"), and move gradually from light to personal, taking turns; escalating mutual disclosure creates closeness between strangers (Aron et al., 1997).
- Two sentences max about work or side projects; listen more.
- If asked what he's looking for, answer simply and honestly.
- Kindness and intelligence are the top-ranked traits for both sexes across 45 countries (~14,000 people, 2020): show them.

### After the first date (the date matters more than the text)

- **Next day around noon**, three parts: warm hello, a specific detail or inside joke from the date, test the waters for a second date. "{Name}! Had a great time last night. Hope you survived that second cocktail. I'd love to see you again, OK if I text in a couple of days to set something up?"
- Or the same night: "I had fun tonight. Let me know when you're home safe :)".
- **Second invite** a few days later, as specific as the first (day, place, activity).
- **Between dates** he doesn't need to keep a conversation going. If she writes, answer and match her energy.

### Keeping momentum

- Keep it contextual and balanced. Pull threads from what she shared ("How did the interview go?!"), ask for recommendations ("Any album I should know? Long drive tomorrow"), share his life (a photo of what he cooked, a gig).
- Upgrade the platform: suggest a call or a video chat; send a link to a plan ("This looks great and it's under an hour away, this weekend?"); invite her to join when he's already out with friends.
- Bigger plans (a weekend trip) only once things are clearly going well.
- Exclusivity is discussed in person, not by text. If she sets a boundary ("only with someone exclusive"): "I respect that."
- **Flirting and spicier texting**: lead in person first. By text only after real physical closeness, always at the level she sets, and never pushy. Consent and respect apply in every channel.

### When things go wrong ("fool me once")

| Situation | Response |
| --- | --- |
| She stopped replying | One light nudge: "Little nudge: I'm still curious about your {X} story :)" Then let go |
| She says it's not a fit | "Thanks for telling me straight, I appreciate it. Good luck out there :)" |
| "Not a good time" | "Understood. I enjoyed talking with you, my door's open if things change." |
| Keeps declining to meet | "I like talking with you, but I'd rather get to know you in person than by text. If you'd like that, I'm happy to plan something." |
| Cancels day-of with a reason | "No problem, how about later this week?" |
| Cancels with no reason | "Shame, I was looking forward to it. Happy to reschedule if you want." A second cancellation: stop |
| "Too tired" / "swamped" | Light, no pressure: offer a 20-minute coffee near her, once |
| Things move too fast for her | "Fair enough, I'm just enjoying this. Let's take it slower: coffee and a walk later this week?" Wait at least 3 days |
| Scared to get invested | Acknowledge, reassure, suggest talking by phone |
| Busy but clearly wants to see him | "Totally get it. I'll check in with you in two weeks." Wait at least 2 weeks |
| Conflict in a relationship | Move off text: "Let's talk this through by phone or on a walk tonight." |

### Rekindling

Use serendipity and shared experiences: she posts an activity he loves, they turn out to live in the same city, she travels somewhere he knows (send one useful recommendation, then offer to catch up when she's back and follow up slightly later than promised), or he passes the bar of their first drink. One message, one concrete proposal, accept the answer. After a bad ending, wait months, then reach out with a genuine memory and what has changed.

## 7. Instagram (after the dating app works)

Dating on Instagram is like dating on apps without guardrails: it doesn't tell you who's single, and social proof matters more. Cold DMs to strangers almost never work. Start building now, approach only once app matches are regular; expect 3-6 months.

| Element | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Profile picture | Same standard as the hero photo |
| Bio line | The best hook, one line |
| First 6-9 posts ("above the fold") | All high-scoring; pin 3 posts to control the top of the grid; carousels mixing him + ambience shots |
| Captions | Mini personal statements: why this moment matters, 2-3 sentences |
| Social proof | At least one group photo; engage with friends' posts so they engage back. Likes and followers matter little, but some engagement is needed |
| Ignore | Verification (try-hard if not famous), link in bio, story highlights, follower count |

- Separate personal and professional accounts: the personal one should show a person, not a storefront.
- Rhythm: one quality post per week fills the grid in about two months. Good profiles reflect a real lifestyle.
- **Qualify before writing:** single (no partner in recent photos or tags)? Compatible (same area, close lifestyles)? Existing link (mutual followers, same scene)? No link → don't DM strangers.
- **Approach:** reply to a story with a "why now" reason (she's at a concert he knows, a trail he's done). One contextual message that makes her smile; no date request in the first message; no repeated follow-ups.

## 8. Small markets, reputation and boundaries

- **Estimate the pool:** population × share in the target age band × ~50 % × share single × share on the app × share within the radius. In an island or small town, this can fall to a few thousand people: profiles repeat fast, and every first impression "spends" part of the pool. Launch only when photos and words are ready.
- **Transient profiles** (people relocating for work, students, tourists): they want to discover the area. Local knowledge is an asset: propose a walk, a spot, a concert.
- **Car-dependent areas:** propose halfway places.
- **Reputation:** in small communities, everyone talks. Stay classy everywhere, message few people, never date two people from the same close circle in parallel, never discuss dates with mutual acquaintances.
- **Professional boundaries:** no dating contact with clients, students, former students, patients or their parents, on any platform.
- **Second app:** only once the first is exhausted; check which apps are actually used locally.
- **Genuinely small pool:** accept a smaller pool, widen the radius on trips, or spend time where more people are. People complain about dating in every city, so check the profile before blaming the market.
- **Tougher on apps:** some groups get fewer right swipes (OkCupid reported it for South Asian men in the US; height filters penalise shorter men). Play to strengths, use humour on visible traits, and don't rely only on apps.
- **Don't rely only on apps.** About 39 % of US heterosexual couples met online in 2017, versus 2 % in 1995 (Stanford data via Statista): the first channel, not the only one. Recurring offline places (clubs, classes, jam sessions, sport) create the mutual connections Instagram needs and the community many users are also looking for. Apps' group features (Double Date, events) help too.

## 9. Plan, KPIs and weekly review

**Timeline template (no reset):**

| Week | Work |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Audit, data export, reset decision, photo sorting |
| 1-2 | DIY photo session, bio and prompts drafts, book photographer, friend feedback |

With a reset, everything after the shoot shifts by at least 3 months; use the wait to perfect photos and words, or launch on another app.

**KPI dashboard**

| Indicator | Benchmark (men, Tinder) | Target week 4 | Target week 8 | If below |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Match rate (matches ÷ likes sent) | Average 5.3 %, median 2 %, top 10 % 12.5 % | 5 %+ | 10 %+ | Change the hero photo first, then photos 2-3 |
| Matches passing 5 messages | ~26 % | 35 % | 45 % | Rework icebreakers and connection messages |
| Dates per month | No reliable benchmark | 2 | 4 | Invite earlier, with day + place + activity |
| Hero photo rating | Practitioner threshold 8/10 | 8+ | — | New shoot |

**Sunday review (10 minutes)**

- Log likes sent, matches, conversations, dates.
- Change one variable at a time (one photo or one line), keep it at least 7 days before judging.
- Monthly: export app data to check the real numbers.
- Note what worked in messages; reuse it.

## 10. Evidence base

| Finding | Practical consequence | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Faces judged in 100 ms; longer exposure changes little (Willis & Todorov, 2006) | Hero photo decides most | Psychological Science |
| Strangers pick better photos of you than you do (White et al., 2017, 600+) | Outsource photo choice | Cognitive Research |
| Photo attractiveness +1.5/7 → +20 % matches; bio improvement → +2 % (Witmer et al., 2025, 445 participants) | Photos first | Global Dating Insights report |
| Happy displays less sexually attractive in men than pride (Tracy & Beall, 2011, 1,000+) | Contained smile on hero | Emotion |
| Smile + direct gaze more attractive; averted gaze favours neutral (Jones et al., 2006) | Match gaze to expression | Psychological Science |
| Cheerleader effect: faces more attractive in groups (Walker & Vul, 2014) | One group photo is worth it | Psychological Science |
| ~10-day stubble most attractive (Dixson & Brooks, 2013, 351 women) | Test stubble | Evolution & Human Behavior |
| Women value humour production (Bressler et al., 2006) | Show humour in text | Evolution & Human Behavior |
| Male-taller norm present in 10,000+ couples, modestly (Stulp et al., 2013) | List height if tall, don't oversell | PLOS ONE |
| Musical ability doesn't increase partners (Mosing et al., 2015, 11,543 twins) | Skills start conversations, don't replace photos | Evolution & Human Behavior |
| Guitar-case field study 31 % vs 14 % (Guéguen et al., 2013) — feasibility contested | Don't cite as proof | Psychology of Music; Times Higher Education critique |
| Rejection mindset grows with profiles seen in a row (Pronk & Denissen, 2020) | Short sessions | Social Psychological and Personality Science |
| People message ~25 % more desirable targets; longer messages don't help; persistence pays (Bruch & Newman, 2018) | Aim high, short specific openers, steady volume | Science Advances |
| Selective desire reciprocated, unselective not (Eastwick et al., 2007, 156 speed daters) | Selective likes, tailored messages | Psychological Science |
| Stated preferences don't predict real choices (Eastwick & Finkel, 2008) | Broad filters | Journal of Personality and Social Psychology |
| Uncertainty about his interest raised attraction (Whitchurch et al., 2011, 47 women) | Restraint early, clarity on invites | Psychological Science |
| Online-to-offline: curvilinear effect of online duration (Ramirez et al., 2015) | Short sequence, then meet | Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication |
| Asking follow-up questions increases liking (Huang et al., 2017) | Follow-ups in text and on dates | Journal of Personality and Social Psychology |
| Escalating mutual self-disclosure builds closeness (Aron et al., 1997) | Light to personal, taking turns | Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin |
| Novel, arousing shared activities raise relationship quality (Aron et al., 2000) | Activity dates from date 2 | Journal of Personality and Social Psychology |
| Kindness and intelligence top-ranked by both sexes, 45 countries (2020, ~14,000) | Show them | Psychological Science |
| Period on short replies read as less sincere (Klin et al., 2015, 126 students) | No full stop on short replies | Computers in Human Behavior |
| Back-to-back texts −20 % likability overall, −26 % under 40 (Lyngo Lab, 805) | One message, not bursts | Lyngo Lab |
| Tinder: men's median message 12 characters, 63 % sent within 5 min (Tyson et al., 2016, ~480k profiles) | Effortful openers stand out | arXiv |
| Tinder users lower body satisfaction; men lower self-esteem (Strübel & Petrie, correlational) | Cap time, judge the profile not yourself | APA convention |
| Smiling vs not: no significant effect, except no smile + no gaze hurts (Photofeeler replication, 7,140 photos) | Avoid the brooding look-away | Photofeeler |

**Limits.** Most studies use North American or European students and lab-rated photos, not real apps in the user's city. Platform numbers are correlations from companies with products to sell; Hinge's photo figures date from 2017. Some widely shared figures have no traceable source (height percentages, "+50 % if you reply within 24 h", "58 % for personalised messages") and must be labelled as such. The user's own weekly data outranks everything here.

## Guardrails

- Honest self-presentation only: true photos, true facts, no fake profiles, no AI faces.
- Respect and consent in every message; no pressure, no negging, no pickup-artist manipulation; accept a no gracefully.
- Adults only. Never help target minors or anyone who hasn't opted in (no scraping, no stalking across platforms).
- Safety: public first dates, tell a friend, verified profiles.
- Wellbeing first: cap app time; if the user seems distressed by rejection, address that before optimising numbers.
- Not legal, medical or therapeutic advice.
